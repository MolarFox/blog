## A Kobayashi Maru?
A no-win situation - a test! In the Star Trek, The Wrath of Khan, we see Starfleet cadets undergo this test - commanding a simulated ship towards inevitable, crushing defeat in an unwinnable scenario.

Although the outcome will always be crushing defeat, the test aims to assess _how_ cadets handle such a situation. Do they make decisions well? Do they handle the situation with grace? Do they minimise their losses, despite the unfavourable outcome?

Today's scenario falls firmly into this category, and we'll focus on identifying and responding to it through this lens.

# Thinking Tactically
Prevention is the best countermeasure for tough situations at work. 

To that end, let's look at how you can develop your tactical mindset when it comes to politics, without losing your sanity! 

## What's with all the Chess?
Ah yes, you'll see a lot of chess references in this post! Those references can be skipped without detracting too much from the content, if you wish. 

The point of them is to avoid thinking emotionally about a situation, and instead see cause and effect in a tactical way. 

## The Ladder of Inference
At the end of the day, "tactical awareness" in a political sense mostly comes from your ability to tie the actions of people around you to their motives, needs, and larger picture intentions. 

The ladder of inference is a tool that can help you to do this. The intention behind it is not to change your thoughts necessarily, just to make you more conscious of how you think, and how you're drawing conclusions from observations you make

### How it works
We step through each run of a ladder. 

1. **Selected Reality** - Specific and objective facts / observations
2. **Interpretations** - Your immediate interpretation of those facts
3. **Assumptions** - Assumptions you've drawn on, in addition to the facts
4. **Conclusions** - The conclusion your interpretation and assumptions have led you to
5. **Beliefs** - How that conclusion has affected your thinking / feelings
6. **Actions** - The actions you take as a result

Different online resources have slightly inconsistent steps and ways of describing those steps to one another, some being a little too vague or buzzwordy to be of practical use. Like most things theory, it tends to make more sense with something more tangible.

### Where we'll use it
Being able to reframe the deluge of information you receive day-to-day in this way helps you to both focus in on the important observations, and then lean more on logic than emotion to analyse them. 

Throughout the rest of this post, I'll aim to be pretty intentional about structuring my paragraphs in the shape of our ladder here.
