We're going to walk through the mindset you'll use to recognise the mechanics of some of the more exasperating experiences almost all of us have to go through in our careers, how you can use that understanding to make good decisions, and how to keep yourself sane through it all.

# What Does "Politics" Actually Mean?
Politics is all about power dynamics in an organisation. The connotation is always negative, and when people refer to it in conversation, it almost always is about the kind of political plays that disingenuously invoke some normal process or communication for its side-effects.

Scheduling an important sync-up meeting when someone is planning to be on personal leave, in order to make them look bad for their absence, is one common example. Loudly praising someone to indicate they deserve to lead an important project, quietly knowing that the project is likely to fail soon, is another.

# The Concept of Incentive Traceability
Being able to recognise asymmetries in the apparent vs actual nature of peoples' intentions and actions will give you the awareness to make better informed decisions.

## Questions to Interrogate Intent
These are questions you can ask about _a person_.

<!-- TODO - tailor this more to Qs actually used in main article later -->

- What does this person want in the **short term**? 
- What drives this person in the **longer term**? 
- What are they **prepared to sacrifice** to get what they want? (negotiable factors)
- What will they **not allow to be taken** from them? (non-negotiable factors)
- How does this person **typically operate** and communicate?
    - How does this change in different circumstances, or when specific people are present?

## Questions to Interrogate Actions
These are questions you can ask about _an action_ a person has made.

<!-- TODO - tailor this more to Qs actually used in main article later -->

- What is the **direct impact** of this action?
- **What else changes** due to this action?
- Can this action **be reversed easily**?
- Does anything else **implicitly** need to happen now?
    - **Who** needs to do that thing?
    - **Are they forced** to do what they need to do, or can they decline?
- How can this action **be perceived** by different people?

## Lining Up Intent and Action
Once we've finished understanding what the person we're observing wants, and what their actions can get them, we can connect the dots between the two.

We can apply this anywhere and to any action + person, but it becomes particularly useful when your instincts tell you something seems intangibly "off" about a situation. Asking these quetions help make tangible the contradictions between action and intent that may have given you that feeling.

## Takeaways from Incentive Traceability
Being able to think this way is a key skill that you aren't likely to be directly taught, but that you will need to develop over the course of your career.

The process of confronting situations in the broader category of workplace theatre can feel pretty cynical, especially early on in your career when it's not yet based on lived experience to make it feel justified. 

The skill behind the skill here is being able to do these analyses whilst keeping your enjoyment of work, your sanity, and (importantly) your moral compass intact!

Let's get into some examples. These are some common Qs I've worked through with my junior engineer mentees in the past.


# Why Is Nothing Happening About My Situation?
We're going to dive right into one of the most brutal things you may go through.

In any conflict, there's always multiple perspectives - many of which you have limited insight into. Therefore it's always a good idea to start by reflecting on what your role in that conflict is, and how you could change your behaviour to improve the situation.

To underscore the point here though, let's remove that nuance.

## Working With Dr Evil
### Situation
Ok here's the deal - you work with Dr Evil, and he's decided that his next mastermind scheme will be directed at you. He's doing all sorts of overtly villainy things to you.
- He's been claiming your ideas as his own
- He spreads nasty rumours about you with your senior leaders
- One time he invited you to his volcano lair and shared all his childhood trauma with you, telling you you'll never be a mastermind like him because you haven't been through the same hardships
- Whenever he disagrees with you during standup, he moves an orbital laser over your house and blows a hole into your roof

### Escalating
One day Dr Evil shoots a hole into the roof of your bathroom whilst you're having a shower, and you decide enough is enough. Orbital lasers tend to be pretty unsubtle, and you go to someone in leadership to talk about what's been happening. 

Usually this will be your manager / the person you report to.

### Nothing Happens
In your one-on-one, your manager listens attentively. They look at the pictures of your swiss cheese roof, and thank you for bringing this up to them. They then ask you to try and make a better effort to work with Dr Evil so he stops blowing holes in your roof, and mentions that you sometimes just need to follow the instructions of your seniors a little better. "That's life".

No action is taken after your one-on-one, and your manager grows frustrated with you whenever you bring up the topic again. 

As an aside, it's worth noting that we'll be talking about simplified worst case scenario in a lot of these examples. This response from a manager is a common occurance, but **should not a normal one**. In a healthy environment you should expect that your manager / whoever you speak to is prepared to hear you out and investigate issues. 

## Applying Incentive Traceability
### What Might Our Manager Want?
- Short term: to remove the internal conflict hapening in the team
- Long term: for the team to operate in harmony towards team productivity goals
- Long term: to upskill and develop members of the team


### What might this response do?
- Directly assigns you in charge of finding a resolution to the problem
- Prevents follow-ups - future one-on-ones should at most be about helping you to find that resolution

### Connecting Intent to Action
In this simple example, we should recognise that the main goal of our manager is to make this conflict go away. It's good to bear in mind that conflict in general can have poor optics for everyone implicated - it's bad for you and Dr Evil, it reflects poorly on your manager's leadership, and depending on severity it can reflect poorly on your organisation too.

In an ideal world, conflict should be resolved, not just removed - but in an objective lens, the incentive for most involved is just for it to go away.

The short answer to why nothing may be happening about your situation may be that doing nothing introduces less liability than putting a spotlight on what is happening.

This is just an example - given the information available to you, you can use the guiding questions to trace incentive in a more specific way to your own circumstances.

### "That's life"
In this post, something I hope you take away is that understanding situations you are in is always the most important first step - making decisions comes later!

Sometimes that decision may be to do nothing for now. Acknowledging that your only options carry too much risk of making the situation worse, and doing nothing is the best choice. In this part, building understanding is the only concern we have for now. We'll talk about making decision later.

One thing I want touch on here is the sentiment of "that's just life" you are likely to hear often in your career. It's a coping statement - that even if things are a little unfair sometimes, there are issues that just aren't worth pursuing. This is part of the advice that I give you via this post as well.

When you are told this, it is usually told with good intentions by someone with lived experience - in the case of our fake scenario here, it's good advice to brace young engineers for the reality of the work.

_However_ - when this sentiment comes from someone in your leadership team who _does_ have the ability to pursue and deal with that issue, it's a negative sign. Just be mindful that it's common for sentiments like these to be used to justify inaction.
