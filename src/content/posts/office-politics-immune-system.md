# What Does "Politics" Actually Mean?
Politics is all about power dynamics in an organisation. The connotation is always negative, and when people refer to it in conversation, it almost always is about the kind of political plays that disingenuously invoke some normal process or communication for its side-effects.

Scheduling an important sync-up meeting when someone is planning to be on personal leave, in order to make them look bad for their absence, is one common example. Loudly praising someone to indicate they deserve to lead an important project, quietly knowing that the project is likely to fail soon, is another.

# The Concept of Tactical Foresight
Being able to recognise asymmetries in the apparent vs actual nature of peoples' intentions and actions will give you the awareness to make better informed decisions.

## Questions to Interrogate Intent
These are questions you can ask about _a person_.

<!-- TODO - tailor this more to Qs actually used in main article later -->

- What does this person want in the **short term**? 
- What drives this person in the **longer term**? 
- What are they **prepared to sacrifice** to get what they want? (negotiable factors)
- What will they **not allow to be taken** from them? (non-negotiable factors)
- How does this person **typically operate** and communicate?
    - How does this change in different circumstances, or when specific people are present?

## Questions to Interrogate Actions
These are questions you can ask about _an action_ a person has made.

<!-- TODO - tailor this more to Qs actually used in main article later -->

- What is the **direct impact** of this action?
- **What else changes** due to this action?
- Can this action **be reversed easily**?
- Does anything else **implicitly** need to happen now?
    - **Who** needs to do that thing?
    - **Are they forced** to do what they need to do, or can they decline?
- How can this action **be perceived** by different people?

## Lining Up Intent and Action
Once we've finished understanding what the person we're observing wants, and what their actions can get them, we can connect the dots between the two.

We can apply this anywhere and to any action + person, but it becomes particularly useful when your instincts tell you something seems intangibly "off" about a situation. Asking these quetions help make tangible the contradictions between action and intent that may have given you that feeling.


### Where This Comes From
In chess there is the concept of "vision" - being able to recognise what your opponent is playing for (eg: stalemate, checkmate, low ELO opportunistic captures, etc), and seeing a few moves into the future to predict what might happen after your next move.

I've just renamed "vision" to "foresight" as vision can be a pretty overloaded term in isolation.

From this position, black can play `Qe6+`, forcing white to take their queen with `Bxe6` to escape check.

If our vision goes a little further however we can see that this is actually a queen sacrifice that forces white's bishop out of the way of black's rook, allowing a neat checkmate in 2 with the followup `Re7#` defended by black's dark bishop.

If this is all feeling a little theoretical so far, don't worry, things only get more practical from here!

## Takeaways from Tactical Foresight
Being able to think this way is a key skill that you aren't likely to be directly taught, but that you will need to develop over the course of your career.

The process of confronting situations in the broader category of workplace theatre can feel pretty cynical, especially early on in your career when it's not yet based on lived experience to make it feel justified. 

The skill behind the skill here is being able to do these analyses whilst keeping your enjoyment of work, your sanity, and (importantly) your moral compass intact!
